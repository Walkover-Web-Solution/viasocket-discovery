Resend vs Loops vs Plunk: what they're like to use

All three send transactional and marketing email. The difference is who they are built for: Resend the developer, Loops the SaaS lifecycle marketer, Plunk whoever wants control of the stack.

Resend

Resend feels built around the API call: verify a domain, grab a key, pick an SDK and send, with templates written in React Email. Marketing features exist but bill separately from sending.

What I liked

  • Official SDKs in nine languages, plus a CLI.
  • Hosted MCP server and an agent plugin, per Resend.
  • Pro at $20 covers 50,000 emails with a flat overage rate.

What I didn't

  • The free plan stops at 100 emails a day.
  • Marketing contacts are a separate bill, from $40 for 5,000.
  • Self-serve plans are monthly only, with 30-day data retention.

Best for: developers who want a clean transactional API and are happy to build the product emails themselves.

Loops

Loops is aimed at whoever runs onboarding and winback emails, not just the engineer. All email types share one contact record, you design in its editor, and the bill follows subscribed contacts.

What I liked

  • Workflows branch on audience filters, with themes and reusable components.
  • Paid plans send unlimited transactional and marketing email.
  • Integrations with Stripe, Supabase, Clerk and Zapier, plus an MCP server.

What I didn't

  • Pricing is by contact, and the page shows one paid tier.
  • Emails are designed inside Loops, so API calls pass data variables, as one Bubble forum user found.
  • The free plan adds a Loops footer and caps combined sends.

Best for: SaaS teams that want marketing and product email on one contact record without per-send fees.

Plunk

Plunk has the simplest bill and is the only one you can run yourself. Transactional, campaigns, workflows and inbound routing sit in one product. Self-hosting is the real work: Docker, a domain and your own AWS SES account.

What I liked

  • Pay per email with unlimited contacts and no base fee.
  • Self-host with Docker under AGPL-3.0.
  • Transactional, workflows and inbound email in one tool.

What I didn't

  • Self-hosting needs SES and SNS setup, and new SES accounts start in sandbox.
  • SDKs and MCP are not listed on its site.
  • The free plan carries Plunk branding.

Best for: cost-conscious teams, or those who need to own the stack, and can run email infrastructure.

Which to choose

  • Choose Resend if your engineers own email and you want a clean API, many SDKs and React templates.
  • Choose Loops if product and marketing run onboarding emails and you want one contact record without per-send fees.
  • Choose Plunk if you want pay-per-email pricing or need to self-host, and can manage AWS SES.

Count your contacts as well as your sends before you decide: Loops and Resend marketing bill on contacts, Plunk and Resend sending bill on volume.

FAQ

Is Loops transactional email free?

On Loops paid plans, transactional email is included at no extra charge. On the free plan it shares a 4,000-send cap with marketing email per rolling 30 days.

What is Resend's free plan limit?

Resend Free allows 3,000 emails a month, capped at 100 a day. Paid plans have no daily limit.

Can I self-host Plunk?

Yes. Plunk is AGPL-3.0 and runs from a Docker image. Its docs also require a domain and an AWS SES account.

Which of these has an MCP server?

Resend lists a hosted MCP server and a CLI. Loops lists an MCP server, a CLI and agent skills. Plunk's site does not list an MCP server.
