## Short answer
Hume announced that its TTS and EVI APIs close 13 November, with account data deleted after. ElevenLabs, Cartesia and Deepgram are three common Hume AI alternatives. ElevenLabs covers 90+ languages at $0.08 an agent minute. Cartesia targets low-latency agents at $0.03 a minute. Deepgram pairs speech-to-text with TTS at $0.030 per 1,000 characters.
Hume gives no migration guide, only a link to its Real World VoiceEQ comparison of voice models, so export prompts, configuration and voice settings before the cutoff.

## ElevenLabs vs Cartesia vs Deepgram: what they're like to use
By Ragini Mahobiya. None of these Hume EVI alternatives is a drop-in copy of EVI, so the right pick depends on what you used Hume for: expressive voices, cheap real-time agents, or listening as much as speaking.

### ElevenLabs
ElevenLabs is the broadest platform here: TTS in 90+ languages, cloning, speech-to-text and an agent product for phone, chat and WhatsApp. If Hume's expressive delivery was the draw, audio tags are the closest match I found.
What I liked:
- Voice realism that independent benchmarks rate highly.
- Audio tags such as [whispers] for expressive delivery.
- The most languages here, 90+ on v4.
What I didn't:
- Pricing mixes credits, characters and minutes, which confuses some users.
- The highest agent rate of the three, at $0.08 a minute.
- No self-hosting; data residency is Enterprise only.
Best for: products where voice quality, cloning and language coverage matter more than unit price.

### Cartesia
Cartesia is built for real-time voice agents, and the pricing shows it: the lowest agent rate here and one credit per character for TTS. Its emotion control is beta and English only, so test it first if expressiveness is why you chose Hume.
What I liked:
- $0.03 an agent minute, the lowest list rate here.
- Clones a voice from a 10-second clip.
- Speed, volume and emotion controls in the API.
What I didn't:
- Emotion control is beta and English only.
- Free plan concurrency is 2 TTS streams.
- An independent phone-agent benchmark measured 244ms, against the claimed 90ms.
Best for: teams building phone agents where cost per minute and response speed matter most.

### Deepgram
Deepgram comes from speech-to-text, and that is still its edge: one vendor for listening and speaking, with a self-hosted option for regulated teams. Its TTS is the most limited of the three on languages and expression.
What I liked:
- Speech-to-text and TTS from one vendor on one bill.
- $200 of free credit that does not expire.
- Self-hosting in your VPC or on-prem on Enterprise.
What I didn't:
- Aura-2 covers 7 languages, and Flux TTS starts English only.
- No voice cloning yet.
- No emotion controls, a gap for teams leaving Hume.
Best for: teams that need accurate transcription and TTS together, or must self-host.

## Which to choose
- Choose ElevenLabs if voice quality, cloning and language coverage are the priority.
- Choose Cartesia if you run high-volume phone agents and want the lowest per-minute rate.
- Choose Deepgram if transcription accuracy, one vendor for both directions or self-hosting matter most.

## FAQ
Q: When do Hume's TTS and EVI APIs shut down?
A: Hume's notice says access ends 13 November at 12:01 a.m. EST, and data in your account is permanently deleted after that.
Q: Which Hume alternative costs least for voice agents?
A: On list rates, Cartesia's Managed Agents cost $0.03 a minute, against $0.075 for Deepgram's Standard Voice Agent and $0.08 for ElevenLabs. Each bundles LLM and telephony differently, so compare the full bill.
Q: Which Hume EVI alternative has emotion control?
A: ElevenLabs uses audio tags such as [whispers]. Cartesia has emotion, speed and volume controls, with emotion in beta for English only. Deepgram has no emotion controls today.
Q: Does Deepgram support voice cloning?
A: Not today. Trade press on the Flux TTS launch says Deepgram plans voice cloning and finer emotional controls, with no date.
Q: Can I self-host a Hume alternative?
A: Deepgram offers self-hosted containers on Enterprise, and Cartesia offers on-prem or VPC deployment through sales. ElevenLabs lists no self-hosted option.
