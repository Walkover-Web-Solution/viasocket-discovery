Devin vs OpenHands vs OpenComputer: what they're like to use

These three differ less in what they can do and more in who runs them. Devin is a managed service, OpenHands is a toolkit you operate, and OpenComputer is a Slack teammate you can host yourself.

Devin

Devin is the polished, managed choice. You sign in, connect your tools and hand it work, and the plan handles models and quotas for you.

What I liked

  • Works from Slack and Teams, with Linear and Jira links.
  • Connects to GitHub, GitLab and Bitbucket.
  • Devin Bots give always-on roles on Max and Teams.

What I didn't

  • Cloud agents start at the Pro plan, not Free.
  • Usage past your allowance bills at API pricing.
  • Your own VPC deployment is an Enterprise option.

Best for: teams that want a hosted agent with SSO and vendor support, and no servers to run.

OpenHands

OpenHands is the most open of the three. Its Agent Canvas runs the OpenHands agent or other agents on your laptop, in Docker or on your own VMs.

What I liked

  • MIT license and a very large community, 94,356 stars.
  • Runs Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI, Pi and OpenCode too.
  • You choose the model and keep the keys.

What I didn't

  • Teams starts at $500 per month for 10 users.
  • Teams is capped at 5,000 sessions per month.
  • Even paid Teams is self-hosted, so you operate it.

Best for: engineering teams that want control over models, data and where agents run.

OpenComputer

OpenComputer is the newest and the most Slack-native. You mention @Computer in a thread, and each thread becomes a session that ends in a pull request.

What I liked

  • Bring your Claude or ChatGPT plan, or use theirs at cost.
  • Anyone on a thread's people list can steer the session.
  • Self-host on AWS, Azure or Google Cloud for free.

What I didn't

  • Slack is the main front door, so it fits Slack teams best.
  • New sessions start in a mode with no approval prompts.
  • A young project, with 1,784 GitHub stars.

Best for: Slack-first teams that want fast pull requests and a cheap, self-hostable start.

Which to choose

  • Choose Devin if you want a managed agent with SSO, vendor support and no infrastructure.
  • Choose OpenHands if you want MIT-licensed software, your own models and the choice of which agent runs.
  • Choose OpenComputer if your team lives in Slack and wants pull requests from a thread at a low entry price.
  • Want a fixed monthly price? Devin and OpenComputer both list a $20 plan, while OpenHands Teams is $500.

Whichever you pick, start with one repository and a few well-scoped tasks before rolling out to the team. Comparing the sandboxes agents run in? See E2B vs Daytona vs Modal.

FAQ

Is OpenComputer really an open-source alternative to Devin?

OpenComputer describes itself that way, under Apache-2.0, and you can self-host it. It is a newer, Slack-first project, so check that its workflow fits your team.

Is OpenHands free?

The open-source edition is free under the MIT license and runs with your own LLM key. The Teams plan is $500 per month for 10 users, and Enterprise is custom.

How much does Devin cost?

Devin lists Free at $0, Pro at $20 per month, Max at $200 per month and Teams at $80 per month. Enterprise is custom.

What is a work minute on OpenComputer?

It is the time the agent spends working on your message, such as reading code and running tests. Waiting on you is free, and extra minutes cost $0.01 each.

Can I use my own model with these?

OpenHands uses your own LLM key. OpenComputer can use your Claude or ChatGPT plan or an API key. Devin includes models in its plans and lets you buy extra usage at API pricing.
