## Short answer
Bruno, Hoppscotch and Yaak are MIT-licensed API clients and Postman alternatives. Bruno is an offline desktop app that keeps collections as files in Git. Hoppscotch runs in the browser, desktop and CLI with a self-host option. Yaak is a desktop app, free for personal use, with a paid license for work.

## Bruno vs Hoppscotch vs Yaak: what they're like to use
By Jatin Kashiv. All three replace a heavier client, but they make different bets: Bruno on Git, Hoppscotch on the web, and Yaak on a focused desktop app with one-time licensing.

### Bruno
Bruno is built around the idea that your collections are just files. Requests live in a folder you can commit, and the project says there will be no cloud sync.
What I liked:
- Collections are plain files, so Git is the sync layer.
- HTTP, REST, GraphQL and gRPC in the free edition.
- Clear tiers at $6, $11 and $18 per user per month.
What I didn't:
- Free is limited to 2 workspaces and 5 OpenAPI syncs a month.
- Push, branching and merge tools in the Git UI are paid.
- No cloud sync by design, so sharing goes through Git.
Best for: teams that want API collections in Git next to their code.

### Hoppscotch
Hoppscotch is the lightest to start with. You can open it in a browser, install the desktop app or use the CLI, and choose cloud or self-hosted workspaces.
What I liked:
- Runs in the browser, on desktop and from the CLI.
- Free plan includes collections, scripts, tests and a runner.
- Business adds MCP server support and API mocking.
What I didn't:
- Team workspaces and shared collections need Business.
- SSO, SCIM and audit logs sit on the $11 Enterprise plan.
- Self-hosting means you run and update it.
Best for: teams that want a web-first client with an optional self-hosted setup.

### Yaak
Yaak is a focused desktop client with a different pricing idea. It is free at home, and you buy a license when you use it at work.
What I liked:
- Covers REST, GraphQL, WebSockets, SSE and gRPC.
- A one-time $349 license gives lifetime commercial use.
- 30-day free trial, activated on first launch.
What I didn't:
- Work use needs a paid license from day one.
- Business is $149 per user per year, the highest here.
- Smaller community than the other two, 19,547 stars.
Best for: solo developers who prefer a one-time purchase over a subscription.

## Which to choose
- Choose Bruno if you want collections as files in Git and no cloud sync.
- Choose Hoppscotch if you want a browser-first client, a self-host option and team workspaces.
- Choose Yaak if you are a solo developer and prefer paying once for a desktop client.
- Cost-sensitive team? At 10 users, Bruno Pro and Hoppscotch Business come to the same yearly price.

## FAQ
Q: Are Bruno, Hoppscotch and Yaak free?
A: All three have a free route. Bruno and Hoppscotch have free open-source or free plans, and Yaak is free for personal use but needs a license for work.
Q: Which is cheapest for a team of 10?
A: Bruno Pro and Hoppscotch Business are both $6 per user per month on annual billing, about $720 a year. Yaak Business is $149 per user per year, about $1,490.
Q: Does Bruno have cloud sync?
A: No. The Bruno README says it is offline-only with no plans for cloud sync. Teams share collections through Git.
Q: Can I self-host Hoppscotch?
A: Yes. Its pricing page lists a Self-Host option next to Cloud, and the project is MIT-licensed on GitHub.
Q: Does Yaak have a one-time license?
A: Yes. The Individual lifetime license is $349 one time and covers commercial use, including future updates.
