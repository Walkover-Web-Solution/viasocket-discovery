OpenRouter vs Vercel AI Gateway vs LiteLLM: what they're like to use

All three get a first request working in minutes. The real choice is who runs the gateway: a marketplace (OpenRouter), a managed control plane (Vercel) or you (LiteLLM).

OpenRouter

The fastest way to try many models: one key, one credit balance, and an OpenAI-compatible endpoint you can point your existing SDK at.

What I liked

  • One balance and one key for 600+ models from 80+ providers, per OpenRouter.
  • Failed attempts are not charged for model tokens.
  • Bring your own key: first $25,000 a month of list-price inference has no fee.

What I didn't

  • The platform fee applies to every credit purchase: 5.5% Standard, 8% Business.
  • Free models are capped at 20 requests a minute and 50 a day.
  • No self-hosted option, so prompts pass through a third party.

Best for: developers and small teams who want the widest model catalog behind one key.

Vercel AI Gateway

Reads like an operations tool more than a model marketplace. It works from any server, not only Vercel, and logs each request with its provider attempts, latency and cost.

What I liked

  • No markup on tokens, including with your own key, per Vercel.
  • Budgets per team, project, API key or team member.
  • Ordered provider and model fallbacks, plus request logs.

What I didn't

  • BYOK needs purchased credits first.
  • Add-ons cost extra, such as $0.10 per 1,000 requests for team-wide zero data retention.
  • Budgets do not cover BYOK spend.

Best for: teams already on Vercel or the AI SDK who want logs and budgets without running a proxy.

LiteLLM

A library and gateway you operate. The Python SDK gives one completion() call across 100+ providers, and the proxy server is an OpenAI-compatible gateway your existing clients can point at.

What I liked

  • MIT core with virtual keys, budgets and rate limits included.
  • Router with retry, fallback and load balancing.
  • Runs in your own environment, so prompts stay with you.

What I didn't

  • You run, secure and upgrade it yourself.
  • SSO, SCIM and audit logs are Enterprise features.
  • Enterprise has no list price; it is sized by annual request capacity.

Best for: platform teams that need control over keys, data and spend inside their own infrastructure.

Which to choose

  • Choose OpenRouter for the broadest catalog and a single prepaid balance.
  • Choose Vercel AI Gateway for budgets, logs and failover with no token markup and no servers to run.
  • Choose LiteLLM if keys, prompts and spend data must stay inside your own infrastructure.

A gateway adds a hop and a dependency, so test latency and failure behaviour with your own traffic before committing.

FAQ

What is the difference between OpenRouter and LiteLLM?

OpenRouter is a hosted service with one balance for many models. LiteLLM is an open-source SDK and proxy that you run yourself with your own provider keys.

Does Vercel AI Gateway charge a markup?

Per Vercel, no. It charges no markup and no platform fee on tokens, including with your own key. Optional add-ons such as reporting and zero data retention are billed separately.

How much does OpenRouter charge?

Inference is billed at provider list price. OpenRouter adds a 5.5% fee on credit purchases on Standard and 8% on Business.

Can I self-host a model gateway?

Yes, with LiteLLM. Its open-source plan is free and self-hosted. OpenRouter and Vercel AI Gateway are hosted services.

Do I need a gateway at all?

Not for one provider. A gateway helps when you use several models, want one set of logs and budgets, or need failover between providers.
