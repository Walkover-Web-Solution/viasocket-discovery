## Neon vs Supabase vs PlanetScale: what they're like to use
By Yashraj Sharma. These three differ in scope. Neon is a database, Supabase is a backend, and PlanetScale is a production database with a free-tier gap.

### Neon
Neon is the most database-focused of the three. Branching and scale to zero make it a good fit for previews and bursty apps.
What I liked:
- Branching is built in, with 10 branches per project on Free.
- Scale to zero keeps idle databases close to free.
- Hosted MCP server with OAuth or API key sign-in.
What I didn't:
- Compute is billed in CU-hours, not a flat fee.
- Free storage is 1 GB per project and 20 GB per account.
- Free compute is capped at 100 CU-hours per project.
Best for: teams that want plain Postgres with branches and pay-for-what-you-use billing.

### Supabase
Supabase is a backend in a box. You get Auth, file storage, Realtime and Edge Functions on top of Postgres, which speeds up a first build. The trade is a flat plan fee.
What I liked:
- One project covers database, auth, storage and realtime.
- Plain Postgres underneath, so leaving is possible.
- Open source, with Docker Compose self-hosting.
What I didn't:
- Free projects pause after a week of inactivity.
- Branching is in beta and needs a paid plan.
- Self-hosting drops branching and managed backups.
Best for: builders who want auth, storage and a database from one vendor.

### PlanetScale
PlanetScale is the production-first option. It has no free tier, but it adds query insights, branching on both engines and MySQL on Vitess from $30 per month.
What I liked:
- Postgres from $15 per month as a three-node HA cluster.
- Insights tab shows query performance.
- Branching on both Postgres and MySQL.
What I didn't:
- No free plan to prototype on.
- Horizontal write scaling for Postgres is not available yet.
- No scale to zero listed.
Best for: teams moving a production database that value uptime and query visibility.

## Which to choose
- Choose Neon if you want plain Postgres, branching and a bill that falls when the app is idle.
- Choose Supabase if you want auth, storage and realtime without wiring other services.
- Choose PlanetScale if you are running production and want HA from $15 plus query insights.
- Need MySQL? Only PlanetScale runs it, on Vitess. Neon and Supabase are Postgres only.
Start on a free plan where one exists, and move once your data size is known. Running background jobs next to the database? See Trigger.dev vs Inngest vs Temporal.

## FAQ
Q: Is Neon cheaper than Supabase?
A: For a small, idle app it can be, since Neon has no monthly minimum and scales to zero. For an always-on app with auth and storage, Supabase Pro at $25 includes more in one bill.
Q: Does PlanetScale have a free plan?
A: Its pricing page lists none. Postgres starts at $15 per month for a three-node cluster, or $5 per month single-node.
Q: Which of the three is open source?
A: Neon and Supabase are Apache-2.0 on GitHub, and you can self-host Supabase with Docker. PlanetScale builds on Vitess, which is Apache-2.0.
Q: Do they all have MCP servers?
A: Yes. Each offers a hosted MCP server. Neon recommends its server for development and testing, and Supabase documents a read-only mode.
Q: Is PlanetScale Postgres or MySQL?
A: Both. PlanetScale runs managed Postgres, and MySQL on Vitess.
Q: Which is best for a free prototype?
A: Neon gives 100 CU-hours and 1 GB per project, and Supabase gives 500 MB across 2 projects, with free projects pausing after a week idle. PlanetScale lists no free plan.
Q: Which has database branching?
A: All three offer it. Neon includes branches on its free plan, Supabase's branching is in beta on paid plans, and PlanetScale offers branching on both Postgres and MySQL.
