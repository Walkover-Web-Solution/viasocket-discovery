Twenty vs Attio vs Folk: what they're like to use

All three replace a spreadsheet or a heavy legacy CRM with something faster. What separates them is ownership, depth and focus: Twenty you own, Attio you model, folk you prospect with.

Twenty

Twenty is the one you shape yourself: custom objects, views, no-code workflows and a TypeScript apps framework, on your own servers or Twenty's cloud. It is the cheapest paid option here, and the trade-off is fewer ready-made integrations.

What I liked

  • Self-host the AGPLv3 core for free, with Docker Compose.
  • Cloud Pro at $9 a seat is the lowest paid price here.
  • REST and GraphQL APIs, webhooks and a native MCP server, per Twenty.

What I didn't

  • No free cloud plan, only a 30-day trial.
  • SSO, audit logs and field-level permissions need Organization at $39.
  • Twenty's own docs say it suits teams that don't need hundreds of prebuilt integrations.

Best for: technical teams that want to own their CRM data and build on it.

Attio

Attio feels like the most polished of the three: a flexible data model, real-time contact sync and enrichment from day one, with Ask Attio, custom agents and workflow agent steps layered on top. The bill climbs fast once you need custom objects.

What I liked

  • A real free plan: 3 seats, 50,000 records, automatic enrichment.
  • App SDK, REST API, webhooks and an MCP server for developers.
  • Agents for research, enrichment and deal risk, built into workflows.

What I didn't

  • Custom objects need Pro, at $79 a seat yearly.
  • AI features draw on credits that run out by plan.
  • Reviewers find integrations and enterprise tooling thinner than HubSpot's or Salesforce's.

Best for: startups and revenue teams that want a modern CRM with agents built in.

folk

Folk is the lightest to adopt: import contacts, grab people from LinkedIn with the Chrome extension, enrich them and send campaigns from one place. It suits relationship-driven teams more than complex data models, and the developer features sit on Premium.

What I liked

  • Enrichment, LinkedIn capture and email campaigns in one tool.
  • Email, calendar and WhatsApp sync on the entry plan.
  • API at 600 requests a minute plus a hosted MCP server.

What I didn't

  • No free plan once the trial ends.
  • API access, custom objects and sequences need Premium at $48.
  • Reviewers report duplicate contacts on Sales Navigator imports and Google-only account sync.

Best for: founders, agencies and small sales teams living in their inbox and LinkedIn.

Which to choose

  • Choose Twenty if you want open source, self-hosting or the lowest seat price, and have developers to build on it.
  • Choose Attio if you want a flexible data model with AI agents and can start on the free plan.
  • Choose folk if your work is relationships and outreach, and you want enrichment and campaigns in one place.

Price the plan you will actually need: custom objects and API access move Attio and folk up a tier.

FAQ

Is Twenty really free?

The AGPLv3 core is free to self-host. Twenty's cloud has no free plan, only a 30-day trial, then starts at $9 a user a month billed yearly.

Does Attio have a free plan?

Yes. Attio Free covers up to 3 seats and 50,000 records, with 3 objects and automatic enrichment. Plus starts at $35 a user a month billed yearly.

Is folk cheaper than Attio?

On entry plans, yes: folk Standard is $24 a member a month yearly against $35 for Attio Plus. For API access and custom objects, folk Premium is $48 against $79 for Attio Pro.

Which of these CRMs has an MCP server?

All three. Twenty includes one in every Cloud workspace, Attio offers one for Claude and ChatGPT, and folk has a hosted MCP server with OAuth.

Can I self-host Attio or folk?

Neither lists a self-hosting option. Of the three, only Twenty can run on your own servers.
